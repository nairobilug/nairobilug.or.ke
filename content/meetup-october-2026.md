### Introduction

The meetup was held in person at Kariokor Social Hall, and celebrated Software
Freedom Day 2026.  We had 8 attendees.  The social hall room we used has many
windows that bring in a lot of ambient light, is well ventilated and can
accomodate upto 120 people.

### Setup

The initial 45 minutes was spent setting up.  The social hall itself does not
provide wifi access, though one can obtain internet access from phone providers
and from local nearby wifi hotspots.  For a local network, we setup a router.
This allowed us to use
[Screen Stream](https://github.com/dkrivoruchko/ScreenStream) from Android
devices as a local service to share screens while presenting.  We also setup a
computer running [Artix Linux](https://artixlinux.org/), with the
[Moksha Desktop](https://github.com/JeffHoogland/moksha) with
[Golly](https://golly.sourceforge.io/), [TuxPaint](https://tuxpaint.org/),
[TuxMath](http://www.tux4kids.com/tuxmath.html) and
[GCompris](https://www.gcompris.net/).  We setup a rotundity projector, but the
brightness (200 ANSI lumens) was not sufficient to enable viewing in the well
light room and the DVI to HDMI cable connecting the computer to the projector
had a loose connection preventing stable display.  In future, if a high lumens
projector is not available, we could request a darkened room to enable use of a
low lumens mini projector, and/or use software tools such as
[Virtual Network Computing (VNC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VNC), 
[OBS Studio](https://obsproject.com), [MediaMTX](https://mediamtx.org/) and
[JanusGateway](https://github.com/meetecho/janus-gateway) to make screen
sharing from one to many devices on a local network more effective and
efficient.

### Golly

[Brian Muhia](https://github.com/poppingtonic) gave an overview of
[Golly](https://golly.sourceforge.io/), a free and open source program that
allows one to explore
[cellular automata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellular_automaton) such as
the [game of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway%27s_Game_of_Life).  The
demonstration was done on an Android phone and streamed to participants.  There
was also a discussion about the use of neural networks in generating cellular
automata.  Attendees tried to use Golly on the GNU/Linux Desktop, but the
projection was not sufficiently visible to enable this to be done effectively.

### Educational software

[Benson Muite](https://github.com/bkmgit) gave an overview of
[Software Freedom Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_Freedom_Day), a
22 year old tradition to celebrate free and open source software.  We also
discussed [Hardware Freedom Day](https://digitalfreedoms.org/en/hfd) and the
possibility of making an open source smartwatch running ZephyrOS, such as
[ZSWatch](https://github.com/ZSWatch/ZSWatch) which features watch faces
created by a Kenyan developer [Felix Biego](https://fbiego.com/).  A short
introduction to TuxPaint, TuxMath and GCompris followed, both TuxPaint and
GCompris have efforts to make them available in Kiswahili.  Next was a live
demonstration showing some of the capabilities of
[Turtle](https://github.com/amrdeveloper/Turtle), an Android program that
allows one to practice programming on CPU and GPU. Attendees tried to use
GCompris on the GNU/Linux desktop, but the projection was not sufficiently
visible to enable this to be done effectively.
