October 2026 Meetup

  • Sat 03 October 2026
  • Meetups
  • Benson Muite
  • #Software Freedom Day, #Cellular automata, #Educational programs

Introduction

The meetup was held in person at Kariokor Social Hall, and celebrated Software Freedom Day 2026. We had 8 attendees. The social hall room we used has many windows that bring in a lot of ambient light, is well ventilated and can accomodate upto 120 people.

Setup

The initial 45 minutes was spent setting up. The social hall itself does not provide wifi access, though one can obtain internet access from phone providers and from local nearby wifi hotspots. For a local network, we setup a router. This allowed us to use Screen Stream from Android devices as a local service to share screens while presenting. We also setup a computer running Artix Linux, with the Moksha Desktop with Golly, TuxPaint, TuxMath and GCompris. We setup a rotundity projector, but the brightness (200 ANSI lumens) was not sufficient to enable viewing in the well lite room and the DVI to HDMI cable connecting the computer to the projector had a loose connection preventing stable display. In future, if a high lumens projector is not available, we could request a darkened room to enable use of a low lumens mini projector, and/or use software tools such as Virtual Network Computing (VNC), OBS Studio, MediaMTX and JanusGateway to make screen sharing from one to many devices on a local network more effective and efficient.

Golly

Brian Muhia gave an overview of Golly, a free and open source program that allows one to explore cellular automata such as the game of life. The demonstration was done on an Android phone and streamed to participants. There was also a discussion about the use of neural networks in generating cellular automata. Attendees tried to use Golly on the GNU/Linux Desktop, but the projection was not sufficiently visible to enable this to be done effectively.

Educational software

Benson Muite gave an overview of Software Freedom Day, a 22 year old tradition to celebrate free and open source software. We also discussed Hardware Freedom Day and the possibility of making an open source smartwatch running ZephyrOS, such as ZSWatch which features watch faces created by Kenyan developer Felix Biego.

A short introduction to TuxPaint, TuxMath and GCompris followed, both TuxPaint and GCompris have efforts to make them available in Kiswahili. GCompris was translated by people from BAKITA in Tanzania as it is used in schools in Tanzania. TuxMath resembles the game Space Invaders, players enter calculations to shoot down projectiles raining on the surface of a planet.

Next was a live demonstration showing some of the capabilities of Turtle, an Android program that allows one to practice programming on CPU and GPU. Attendees tried to use GCompris on the GNU/Linux desktop, but the projection was not sufficiently visible to enable this to be done effectively.

Next Meetup

The next meetup will be held on 13th November as community members are invited to attend OpenInfra Days Kenya on the 6th and 7th of November. Tickets for OpenInfra Days Kenya are available online.
